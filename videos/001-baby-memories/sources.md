# Sources: Why You Can't Remember Being a Baby

- Earliest memories around age 3 to 3.5: Bauer, P. J. (2015), "A complementary processes account of the development of childhood amnesia", Psychological Review. Also Akhtar et al. (2018), Psychological Science: 39% of 6,641 adults claimed memories from age 2 or younger, which the authors say are likely fictional.
- Three-month-olds remember kicking a mobile days later: Rovee-Collier, C. (1999), "The development of infant memory", Current Directions in Psychological Science.
- Yale fMRI study, 2025: Yates, T. S., ... Turk-Browne, N. B. (2025), "Hippocampal encoding of memories in human infants", Science. Hippocampus activity predicted later recognition in infants from about 12 months.
- New neurons rewiring memory circuits: Akers, K. G. et al. (2014), "Hippocampal neurogenesis regulates forgetting during adulthood and infancy", Science (mice).
- Language and labels: Simcock & Hayne (2002), Psychological Science: children rarely describe early events with words they didn't know at the time.
- Lost infant memories switched back on in mice: Guskjolen, A. et al. (2018), "Recovery of 'lost' infant memories in mice", Current Biology (optogenetics).
